# Section 4 — Manitoba Wheat Variety: ranking of E36-E40 vs N36-N40

Criteria: (a) fit to the question-writing rules; (b) coverage of Section 4 skills (pipe, `summarise` + `n()`, `group_by`, multi-step pipelines, `write_csv`); (c) distinctness within the block; (d) usability as an 8-10 minute question graded from a submitted script; (e) pedagogical value.

Context that drives several placements: the existing MB block is a near-copy of the SK block that precedes it in Section 4 (E37 mirrors Q32, E39 mirrors Q34, E40 mirrors Q35), and inside the MB block E38 and E40 are themselves near-duplicates — both group by `Variety`, both compute mean plus `n()`, both sort best-first, and both close on the same small-n caveat. That collision is the single biggest weakness in the existing five.

1. **N38** — `sum(Acres)` grouped by variety is a full pipeline at the right length, and part (c) ("what would totalling `Yield_bu_ac` produce, and why is that number meaningless?") is the best conceptual part in either set: it tests whether the student knows a rate cannot be summed, a genuine and durable error, and it is a purely verbal counterfactual so nothing has to be edited to grade it.
2. **N40** — mutate-then-group-then-summarise-then-`write_csv`, the fullest multi-step pipeline in the block; grouping by municipality keeps it clear of the variety-summary questions, and (c) ties the count back to confidence in the ranking rather than restating a rule.
3. **N39** — the mean column and the count column measuring different things is a real reading skill, and asking for the top row by each forces the student to actually look at their own output instead of reciting a caveat. Distinct from N36/E38 because the comparison, not the ranking, is the point.
4. **E36** — the one question in either set that exercises `filter` + `select` + `arrange` as a pipeline with no grouping, and the "read it aloud as a take-the-data-then sentence" part teaches the pipe as prose. Ranks below the top three only because it tests less of Section 4's grouped-summary core.
5. **N36** — clean `group_by(Municipality)` + mean + `n()`, and (c) asks what two different things could put a municipality on top (better land vs. a better variety mix), which is a real interpretive step beyond "small n is weak". Sits behind E36 because a grouped mean-and-count is the block's most crowded pattern.
6. **E38** — solid canonical `group_by(Variety)` + mean + `n()` + `arrange`, and the n=1 second-place row is a nice built-in trap. Loses ground only to its overlap with E40, which repeats the same pipeline and the same closing point.
7. **N37** — `group_by(Year)` + `summarise(n = n())` alone. Well aimed at the idea that a count is a measurement in its own right, and (c) is sharp, but part (a) spells out the recipe (`group_by` plus `summarise(n = n())`), which the rules say to avoid, and the coding is thin for 8-10 minutes.
8. **E40** — technically the most complete pipeline (filter, group, summarise with `n()`, arrange, `write_csv`), so it earns the `write_csv` slot, but it is E38 plus a year filter and a file write, with a (c) that makes the same small-n argument E38 already made. As written it cannot sit in the same bank as E38 without one of them being redundant.
9. **E39** — the mutate-before-summarise question, and the conversion constant is given in the stem, which is right. But it is Q34 with wheat swapped for lentils, part (c) is the same why-must-mutate-come-first question, and the answer is a rule the student can recite without having run anything.
10. **E37** — a single `summarise(mean, n())` with no grouping at all. It is Q32 with a different file, it is the least Section-4-ish question here (no grouping, no multi-step pipeline), the count part is handed to the student rather than asked for, and it is nowhere near 8-10 minutes of work.

RANKING: N38,N40,N39,E36,N36,E38,N37,E40,E39,E37
