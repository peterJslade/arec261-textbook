Module 1 - Test bank

This bank holds 40 questions: ten in each section.

  • Section 1 covers basic Excel operations
  • Section 2 covers basic descriptive statistics in Excel
  • Section 3 covers Conditional functions and lookup tables
  • Section 4 covers PivotTables

There are two datasets used in the test bank. The datasets are:

  • Saskatchewan RM Crop Yields (1990–2025; long file for PivotTables). Average yield (bu/ac) for eight crops in every Rural Municipality.
  • Manitoba Wheat Variety (2020–2025). Red spring wheat yield by variety and municipality.

Your module test will contain one question per section. The test will only use one of the datasets, and the dataset will be provided in the test.

For each question, 20% of the mark is for presentation and the other 80% is for the correctness of your workbook.

The submitted workbook should have a separate worksheet for each question. Each worksheet should be clearly labelled with the question number. Here is an example of what a test will look like and the expectation for what your workbook will look like:

  • Example test (PDF) – four questions, one per section, Sections 2-4 on the Saskatchewan RM Crop Yields dataset.
  • Example workbook (XLSX) – a completed submission: one labelled worksheet per question, with a live PivotTable for Question 4.

Procedure for test:

  • At least an hour before the test, “Schedule an appointment” in the test for that day.
  • Bring your (charged) laptop to the test.
  • Your test will be accessible at 11:30am.
  • When you get to the room:
  1. Close all applications other than your webbrowser (used to access the test) and Excel.
  2. Log into zoom and join the breakout room with your name on it.
  3. In Zoom, share your screen (not an application window, but your whole screen) and record your session.
  4. Navigate to Canvas and access your test.
  5. Download the dataset that you need
  6. Complete the test in Excel.
  7. Upload the completed workbook to Canvas (while still logged into zoom).
  8. Leave zoom, and upload the screenshot recording to Canvas.
  9. Your done!!

Using the Saskatchewan RM Crop Yields dataset (long file).

(a) Build a PivotTable of average yield by crop for 2023 (Crop on Rows and Yield as Average). Rank the crops from highest to lowest average yield.

(b) Identify the result that is out of line with the others. Explain why it is different and what someone might wrongly conclude from the unfiltered PivotTable.

(c) Add Unit to Filters and select bu/ac. Identify the highest- and lowest-yielding crops now, and explain why this comparison is fairer.

Answer
    1. Lentils ≈1,314, Oats ≈71, Barley ≈55, Spring Wheat ≈43, Peas ≈34, Canola ≈34, Durum ≈30 and Flax ≈20.
    1. Lentils are reported in lb/ac while the other crops are reported in bu/ac. The unfiltered table could make someone think lentils yield about 18 times as much as oats.
    1. After filtering to bu/ac, Oats are highest (≈71) and Flax is lowest (≈20). The values now share the same unit.

    1. Year × Variety grid with Yield set to Average.

(b) Identify the lowest and highest average-yield years and describe the overall movement of the averages.

(c) Explain why a year’s average depends partly on which varieties appear in the data that year and how the Count helps you interpret it.

Answer
    1. Year on Rows, with Yield shown as Average and Count.
    1. The lowest average is 2021 (≈49.6 bu/ac) and the highest is 2025 (≈68.0 bu/ac). The average falls sharply in 2021 and then rises through 2025.
    1. The mix of reported varieties changes between years, so part of the change in the overall average may reflect composition. Count shows how many reported yields support each year’s average.

Answer
    1. Bar PivotChart of variety averages for varieties with at least 30 reported yields.
    1. SY MANNESS is tallest at about 72.1 bu/ac and AAC TISDALE (PT250) is shortest at about 53.9 bu/ac.
    1. The chart makes the ranking and differences in averages easy to see, but it hides the counts and the spread of observations. Starting the bars at zero keeps their lengths proportional to the values.
