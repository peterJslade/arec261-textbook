Module 1 — Full Test Bank

NoteHow this test bank works

This bank holds 120 questions, thirty in each section. Section 1 gives you the data inside the question; Sections 2–4 each have ten questions for each of three datasets. On the real test you receive four questions — one from each section — with the Section 2–4 questions all drawn from the same dataset, and you submit an .xlsx workbook with a separate sheet for each section’s answers. Each question is designed to take about 8–10 minutes.

Expand each Answer block to check your work. Every value has been computed from the real data; when the source files are refreshed the exact numbers may shift slightly, but the method stays the same.

The three datasets.

  • Saskatchewan RM Crop Yields (1990–2025). Average yield (bu/ac) for eight crops in every Rural Municipality. Wide file rm_yields_1990plus.csv for Sections 2–3; long file rm_yields_1990plus_long.csv for Section 4. Blank = crop not grown/reported that RM-year. Lentils are recorded in pounds per acre, everything else in bushels.
  • Manitoba Wheat Variety (2020–2025). Red spring wheat yield by variety and municipality, in mb_wheat_varieties.csv. Reported = FALSE marks a yield suppressed by MASC because too few farms grew that variety there.
  • Canada Field Crops (Statistics Canada, 2015–2025). Seeded acres and average yield for major field crops by province, in statcan_field_crops.csv.

Working with a single year. Several questions ask you to work with one year of the data. Do not just apply a filter and point a formula at the whole column: as Module 1 explains, a filter hides rows from you, not from your formulas. Copy the rows you want onto a new sheet first, or use a conditional function that tests the year. If you copy 2023 out, you will have 295 rows of data.

Grading model. 10% for presentation — labelled cells, sensible formats, a reader can follow the sheet. The remaining 90% is split evenly across the four questions, and evenly across the parts within each question. Parts are marked on follow-through: if an early part is wrong but the later parts are correctly worked from your own earlier answer, the later parts still earn full marks.

Vocabulary note. Everything here uses only Module 1 tools: SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, COUNTA, MEDIAN, MODE.SNGL, MIN, MAX, ROUND, SUMPRODUCT, STDEV.S, VAR.S, QUARTILE.INC, PERCENTILE.INC, IF (including nested), COUNTIF, SUMIF, AVERAGEIF, COUNTIFS, SUMIFS, AVERAGEIFS, XLOOKUP; relative and absolute references; number and date formatting; sorting and filtering; the 1.5 × IQR outlier rule; wide vs long shape; and PivotTables, including PivotCharts and value filters.

Question 1

(b) Add a Total revenue ($) column: revenue per acre times that field’s acres. Format with a thousands separator and no decimals.

(c) Under the table, use SUM to total the acres, the total bushels (yield × acres), and the total revenue.

(d) In a spare column, repeat the (a) formula without the $ signs and fill it down all five rows. Label the block clearly as the incorrect version.

(e) Beside it, write one sentence saying what appears in those cells and why.

    1. Acres 940; bushels 36,637.5; total revenue $520,252.50.
    1. The unanchored version, filled down five rows.
    1. The first row is right; every row below it returns $0.00, because the price reference walks down to F2, F3… which are empty. Excel treats an empty cell as zero here and reports no error, so the column simply fills with zeros.

Question 3

Paste this block into a blank sheet.

Date    Crop    Tonnes  Km hauled
2026-09-14  Canola  42.5    38
2026-09-18  Canola  38.0    52
2026-09-23  Wheat   61.2    27
2026-10-02  Wheat   55.8    64
2026-10-09  Barley  47.3    41

(a) Make sure Excel has read the dates as dates, not text, then format them to show the month as a short name (e.g. 14-Sep-26). Show tonnes to one decimal.

(b) Add a Days since previous column. Leave the first row blank. Make sure the results show as plain numbers.

(c) In one cell, compute the number of days from the first delivery to the last. Use COUNT to confirm how many deliveries there were, and COUNTA to count the entries in the Crop column. Explain in one sentence why COUNT would return 0 on that column.

(d) The hauler charges a base rate of $4.10 per tonne plus $0.085 per tonne for each kilometre, and the elevator then deducts 1.5% of the total for shrinkage. Add a Charge column computing the net charge for each delivery, and total it. Format as currency.

(e) A classmate writes =4.10+0.085*D2*C2*(1-0.015) and gets $139.32 for the first load instead of $306.85. Explain what their formula actually computes, and where the parentheses belong.

(f) In one sentence, explain why part (b) would fail if Excel had stored the dates as text rather than as dates.

Answer
    1. Dates formatted with a short month name (DD-MMM-YY); tonnes 0.0.
    1. =A3-A2 filled down → 4, 5, 9, 7 days. If the cell shows 04-Jan-1900 it inherited the date format; change it to Number.
    1. =A6-A2 = 25 days. =COUNT(C2:C6) = 5; =COUNTA(B2:B6) = 5. COUNT counts only numbers, and the Crop column holds text, so it would return 0 — COUNTA counts any non-empty cell.
    1. =(4.10+0.085*D2)*C2*(1-0.015) filled down → $306.85, $318.90, $385.50, $524.35, $353.39; total $1,889.00.
    1. Without the parentheses Excel applies multiplication before addition, so it computes $4.10 plus (0.085 × km × tonnes × 0.985) — the base rate is added once for the whole load instead of being charged on every tonne. The base and the per-kilometre rate must be grouped first: =(4.10+0.085*D2)*C2*(1-0.015).
    1. Excel stores a date as a number, which is why subtracting two of them gives a count of days. Text cannot be subtracted, so the formula would return an error instead of a gap.

Question 5

Paste this block into a blank sheet.

Field   Acres   Yield (bu/ac)
Willow  213 52.4
Ridge   147 61.8
Slough  326 47.9
Quarter 89  64.5
Airport 255 55.2

(a) Use AVERAGE to find the plain mean of the five yields, and COUNT to confirm how many fields the average was taken over.

(b) Add a Bushels column (yield × acres) and total it, along with the acres, using SUM.

(c) The farm’s actual average yield is total bushels divided by total acres. Compute it. Format both averages to one decimal place.

(d) Compute the same figure a second way, in a single cell, using SUMPRODUCT over SUM.

(e) Add a second Bushels column that uses ROUND to give whole bushels, and total it. Compare that total with the one from (b), then explain in one sentence how rounding each value differs from formatting the column to zero decimals.

(f) The two averages in (a) and (c) differ. In one or two sentences, say which one answers “what did this farm average across all its acres”, and what the plain mean is treating as equally important.

Answer
    1. =AVERAGE(C2:C6) = 56.4 bu/ac; =COUNT(C2:C6) = 5.
    1. =B2*C2 filled down; acres 1,030, bushels 55,677.7.
    1. =D7/B7 = 54.1 bu/ac.
    1. =SUMPRODUCT(C2:C6,B2:B6)/SUM(B2:B6) = 54.1 — the same number in one step.
    1. =ROUND(B2*C2,0) filled down totals 55,678, 0.3 bushels above the unrounded 55,677.7. (The Quarter field lands on exactly 5,740.5; Excel rounds a half away from zero, so it becomes 5,741.)     1. The weighted average in (c) answers it, because it counts every bushel once regardless of which field grew it. The plain mean treats each field as equally important, so the 89-acre Quarter field — the highest yielding of the five — counts as much as the 326-acre Slough field, and pulls the answer up by about 2.3 bu/ac.

(c) Use COUNT to confirm how many plots the summary covers.

(d) In a spare cell, type the range you found in (b) as a number rather than a formula. Label it clearly as the typed version.

Question 12

Paste this block into a blank sheet. Two cells are deliberately empty.

Field   Yield (bu/ac)
Birch   46.9
Coulee  
Draw    52.7
Flats   not seeded
Gully   44.3
Hollow

(a) Use COUNT and COUNTA on the yield column. Report both numbers and explain why they differ.

(b) Use AVERAGE on the yield column. State how many values it used.

(c) Compute the same average a second way, as SUM divided by COUNT, and confirm the two agree.

(d) A colleague suggests typing 0 into the two blank cells “so the column is complete.” Compute what the average becomes, and explain why that is the wrong fix.

(e) State, in one sentence, what a blank cell means here that a zero does not.
